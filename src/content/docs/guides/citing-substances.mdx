---
title: Citing substances
description: One bibitem per (substance, source) - resolution provenance becomes machine-checkable citations.
---

A molecule in a standards document is a *substance with identity*, not
glyphs. AsciiChem closes that loop: resolve an identifier, then emit a
**dataset-type Relaton bibitem** for the source you choose to cite.

## Resolve, then cite

```ruby
substance = AsciiChem::Resolver.resolve(value: "50-78-2", convention: "cas")
xml = AsciiChem::Citation.to_xml(substance)   # Relaton <bibitem type="dataset">
```

From the CLI:

```sh
asciichem cite --cas 50-78-2
```

The bibitem carries the publisher, an accessed date from the
resolution's provenance, a source-specific docidentifier (`PubChem
CID 2244`, `CAS RN 50-78-2`), and **every identifier as a keyword** —
so a citation is machine-checkable against its InChIKey long after
the page numbers change.

## One bibitem per (substance, source)

Two databases agreeing on a substance are still two different
documents. Resolution is multi-source; citation is per-source — pick
the sources you trust, cite each once.

## Sources and licensing

- **PubChem** is the default: keyless, permissive, and the bulk
  mirror of validated CAS RNs.
- **CAS Common Chemistry** is *opt-in*: its data is CC BY-NC 4.0, so
  the adapter never registers itself and every result carries the
  required attribution.

```ruby
AsciiChem::Resolver.register(:common_chemistry,
                             AsciiChem::Resolver::CommonChemistry)
```

Results are cached in your user cache directory (TTL'd, attribution
included); `--refresh` bypasses the cache.
