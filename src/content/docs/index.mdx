AsciiChem is an ASCII syntax for representing **chemistry** — atoms, molecules,
bonds, reactions, electron configurations, and the embedded mathematics
that chemistry publications rely on. It is to chemistry what **AsciiMath** is
to mathematics, but with a semantic model underneath: every parse produces
the same tree, regardless of which front door you came in by.

## One model, four implementations

Four independent packages — `asciichem-rs` (Rust, the authoritative
implementation), `asciichem` (Ruby), `asciichem` (TypeScript), and
`asciichem-py` (Python) — implement the same model and pass the same
[shared corpus](https://github.com/asciichem/asciichem-tests). Every
one of them parses through the same engine: **parsanol over the single
checksummed PARG artifact** of the grammar — there is no per-language
parser left to drift. Pick whichever ships in your stack:

## Why four

A molecule has one structure regardless of the notation you write it in:
InChI, SMILES, molfile, or AsciiChem. Likewise, the *AsciiChem model* has
one shape regardless of the language you run it in. Four implementations
keep the contract honest — and the order among them is explicit:
**asciichem-rs is the authoritative implementation**. When the corpus is
silent, what the Rust crate does is the operating answer; the shared
corpus and the one PARG artifact keep the other runtimes identical to it.
